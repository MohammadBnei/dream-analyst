## P1 × P2 — every UI promise costs a data model

**Stated, not yet worked.** The narration model makes promises that are storage and modelling decisions in disguise:

- « Nothing is lost » — the historique, reversible, with a restore point before each destructive operation.
- The *portée* of a symbole — beat, scène or rêve, changeable after the fact, with inferred occurrences marked *déduit* and removable.
- The diff — additive changes applied silently, modifications proposed with every affected location shown before anything is overwritten.
- The counts — a symbole carries a number across the dreamer's entire history, not just this dream.

None of these have been costed.

### Open — is a *finding* an object, or a fil?

**Hypothesis, one exchange old. Nothing decided.** In the exploration regime (D11) the agent produces a **finding**: a claim about the corpus, such as « trois rêves intenses de la mère précèdent chaque rêve lucide ». The narration model has no vocabulary for it. Rule 7 anchors every *fil* to one of beat, scène, symbole or rêve; a finding is anchored to thirty rêves at once.

```
  fil       1 anchor    dreamer-initiated   conversational
  finding   N anchors   agent-initiated     a claim about the corpus
            ^^^^^^^^
            the only structural difference
```

**Enki's reading (hypothesis):** in the P2 sense a finding looks like a fil. If polymorphic attachment — already named-but-undecided on P2 — is one-to-**many**, they are one shape and a fil is a finding with a single anchor. That generalises an open P2 topic instead of adding one.

Three costs, none resolved:

- **Vocabulary collapse.** Merged on P2 they are still not the same on P1. Rule 7 reads « tout ce qui est conversationnel est un fil ». If findings are fils, that becomes « everything is a fil » and the term stops carrying information. Either P1 keeps provenance visible under a shared P2 shape, or *fil* dies as a term (D6 freezes the vocabulary; it does not protect it from being emptied).
- **Identity across re-derivation.** Ask in March, ask again in May over a corpus grown to forty. Same finding or a new one? A row per ask fragments any refusal history. Stable identity needs a matcher, which is the element-matcher problem one floor up, with the same trap: a wrong merge is permanent and silently attaches an old refusal to a claim that is not the old claim.
- **The anchors move underneath.** Portée widens by speaking (rule 4). So a finding anchored through symboles has a moving anchor set, and is either a **snapshot** (stable identity, goes stale, refusable) or a **live query** (always current, but there is no row to refuse).

## P3 × P1 — every autonomous decision must be refusable

**Partly decided on P1, uncosted on P3.** The agent classifies dimension and operation on its own, proposes modifications, opens fils, infers symboles and marks them *déduit*. The narration model already answers the UX half: nothing destructive applies without consent, and everything inferred is refusable.

**The open tension — latency.** P3's ambitions and P1's narration budget pull in opposite directions. An RLM exploring 100+ dreams is slow and expensive; the narration loop must feel instant, and D4's first premise says the dreamer must never wait. This suggests — does not establish — that the agent editing the dream while you speak and the agent that knows your whole history are different animals meeting at defined points. **Open. Enki's call.** Note that D11 relaxes this only for exploration: in that regime real wait is acceptable, so the tension is a narration tension, not a general one.

### Open — the opposite failure: the agent that is fast and confident

**Hypothesis, one exchange old.** Surfaced by a pre-mortem in the exploration regime. The dreamer wakes lucid and asks what his lucid dreams have in common. The agent answers in four seconds, and is wrong twice without showing it:

```
  the set was guessed     « lucide » is not an object. Not one of the five
                          categories, and no rêve-level property carries it.
                          The agent decided which dreams were lucid, from prose,
                          and no déduit mark exists at rêve level to show it.

  the pattern is a        mère is the densest personnage in the corpus. Any
  base rate               three-night window contains her. Nothing computed
                          what mère-density looks like before a NON-lucid night.

  and it renders like     same typography as « tu as dit : je courais ».
  a recorded fact         Nothing separates recorded from inferred-across-corpus.
                          Rule 8 gives doute to the DREAMER. The agent has no
                          way to say « je crois, faiblement ».
```

Here faster is worse. The written tension above is the agent being slow; this one is the agent being quick and certain, and the eight rules catch neither.

**The damage lands on the corpus, not the screen.** Told the pattern, the dreamer steers his narration toward it and six weeks later the correlation is real — the agent manufactured its own evidence. D4's second premise says the corpus is the ground; this poisons the ground, and the historique records diffs, not why he narrated what he narrated.

### Open — is the durable row the finding, or the refusal?

**Hypothesis, one exchange old. Enki's direction, not decided.** The answer to agent uncertainty is not a confidence scale. **A 1–10 doute was proposed and rejected this session**: it merges dreamer and agent uncertainty in one visual channel, collides with rule 3 (tint means *symbole*, and only that), asserts a precision an RLM cannot calibrate, and costs the dreamer a choice. Instead: the agent proposes, the dreamer refuses, and the refusal is kept and steers later proposals. In the exploration regime the dreamer is an actor and can judge a finding directly by whether it provokes or reads dull.

If findings are **computed** — which P3's direction implies, the corpus being re-explored on each ask — then the finding is ephemeral and the durable row is the **refusal**, and the reaction beside it.

```
  finding carries identity          reaction carries identity
  ─────────────────────────         ─────────────────────────
  findings are rows, matched        findings are free and disposable;
  across re-derivations;            the dreamer's responses are the
  refusals hang off them            corpus's second half

  cost: the matcher, permanent      cost: « what did the agent tell me
  wrong merges, staleness           in March » is unanswerable — only
                                    the answer to it survives
```

**Unresolved cost either way.** Refusal-on-feel trains the agent toward resonance, not truth: a finding that is **true and dull** gets trained out, and what the subconscious most avoids is exactly what will read as dull. And refusal must be non-final, or the first tired « non » closes that space permanently — rule 8's « nothing is lost » has to cover refusals in both directions.
